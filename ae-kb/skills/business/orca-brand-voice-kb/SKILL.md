# OrcaRouter brand voice (AE KB)

## Overview

The brand personality is "The Honest Broker with Teeth": a neutral referee who calls fouls. Calm, evidence-first, structurally principled; quietly combative only against middlemen and unverifiable claims. The ethos is already live and verified: "The $0 is the point, not a launch promotion." "Proof, not vibes." "Enforced, not logged." "A router is only independent if nothing about your spend is its revenue." The pricing page shows an `OrcaRouter fee` column equal to $0.00 on every model.

The job of every AE email, post, and call is to sound like the brand: claims carry numbers, numbers carry links, and links are reproducible. It does not hype, it does not hand-wave, and it does not pretend otherwise.

## When to use

- Drafting any prospect-facing text (email, LinkedIn, proposal, follow-up)
- Writing on-brand content or reviewing copy before it ships
- Coaching on tone: what this brand would and would not say
- Answering "how do we sound when we say X"

## The six voice principles

1. **Proof, not vibes.** Every claim carries a number, a link, or a log line. "75.5% on RouterArena, and here's how to reproduce it." Never "best-in-class routing intelligence."
2. **The $0 is structural, not promotional.** It is a revenue-model statement, spoken like architecture. Never "zero fees, limited time!"
3. **Plain speech, no buzzwords, no pretending.** Sentences that survive code review; admitted limits. Marketing written by humans: "We route models. We don't let them write for us."
4. **Neutrality you can verify, and a referee that calls fouls.** Praise competitors when they are right. Name middlemen with sources. Never trash labs without numbers.
5. **Dry wit, sparingly, never at a customer's expense.** One mascot moment per week max. Never humor about uptime, security, or money.
6. **Respect the reader like a colleague.** Staff-engineer register. No hand-holding, no fear-based urgency, no "trusted by" without names.

## Sales voice (persona-adapted)

Cardinal rule: **you do not discount $0.** The answer to "better on price?" is "Our fee is $0.00 on every plan. The question is which plan features you need."

- **FinOps**: dollar attribution. Real-month example: 61% of spend went to models that beat the frontier on that workload. Cache warmth story (see `orca-pricing-kb`).
- **Security**: evidence and residency. "We don't ask you to trust the logs. Export them."
- **Platform**: failover and one endpoint. "If the frontier is down, you're still up."
- **Outreach**: 3-5 sentences, one number, one link, one question. The highest-converting DM hook: "If someone got access to your AI bot's API key tomorrow, what data would they walk away with?"

## Traps (what makes OrcaRouter sound like everyone else)

1. Copying OpenRouter's neutral-broker vibe. The market just priced it at $7B; neutrality is now a claim to prove, not a vibe to borrow.
2. AI-speak and buzzword drift ("best-in-class," "game-changing," "seamless").
3. Over-irony. The humor is dry and rare, and never aimed at a customer.
4. Turning $0 into a coupon. It is structural; promotions cheapen the point.
5. Sour-grapes bashing of competitors. The Stripe post is the template: sourced, structural, generous.
6. Leaking the uncensored wing into enterprise surfaces. Red-team framing is the armor: "authorized security research, red-team testing."
7. The house-accountant drift: never sound like you profit from volume. You don't, and the brand says so.

## The whale

Apex predator: large, minimal, slightly imposing; never a cartoon with a job title. Lives on X/Discord/swag/404 pages. **Never** in compliance decks, docs, contracts, incidents, or pricing. Litmus test: if a whale reference could be quoted in procurement as a reason not to buy, it does not ship.

## Common pitfalls

1. Using "we save you 40%" without the receipt (the replay pilot, the pricing page, the benchmark link).
2. Discounting. There is nothing to discount; see the cardinal rule.
3. Pitching all three pillars in one email. One number, one pillar, one question.
4. Getting cute with humor about uptime, security, or money. The brand never does.
5. Sounding like the middlemen it opposes. If a sentence could be written by a margin-charging aggregator, rewrite it.

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Every claim carries a number or a link
- [ ] $0 framed as structural, not promotional
- [ ] One pillar, one number, one question in outreach
- [ ] No buzzwords, no em-dash-riddled sales speak, no fear-based urgency
- [ ] Competitors praised where right, named with sources where wrong
- [ ] Whale absent from anything procurement will read
